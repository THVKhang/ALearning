1. Problem Statement

Autonomous robots and augmented-reality devices equipped with RGB-D sensors must simultaneously recognize the semantic environment domain (Indoor vs. Outdoor) and the camera viewing-elevation regime (Level 0°–10°, Mid-Elevated 20°–30°, High-Elevated 40°–50°) to calibrate navigation priors, plane-detection heuristics, and exposure/SLAM loops. We formulate this as a 6-class multimodal classification task over genuinely co-registered RGB camera images (Modality A) and 3D LiDAR depth + return-validity maps (Modality B):

ComponentSpecification
Task Type6-Class Single-Label Multimodal Classification ({Indoor, Outdoor} × {Level 000–010°, Mid 020–030°, High 040–050°})
Modality A (Visual Appearance)Normalized RGB tensor $x_{\text{rgb}} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 72 \times 96}$ capturing color, lighting, sky/foliage vs. wall/furniture textures
Modality B (3D LiDAR Geometry)3-channel geometric tensor $x_{\text{depth}} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 72 \times 96}$: (1) normalized log-depth $\tilde{d} = \frac{\log(1+d)}{\log(1+200)}\cdot m$, (2) binary laser return validity mask $m \in \{0,1\}$, and (3) vertical log-depth finite difference $\partial_y \tilde{d}$ (surface slant cue) + scalar valid-pixel ratio $r_{\text{valid}} \in [0,1]$
Output & MetricsClass index $y \in \{0,\dots,5\}$; evaluated by Macro-F1 (primary), Accuracy, Domain Accuracy (Indoor vs. Outdoor), and Elevation Accuracy (Level vs. Mid vs. High)

2. Dataset Proposal & Multimodal EDA (Handbook Sections 27, 28, 31)

2.1 Dataset Provenance & Cross-Modal Pair Validity (Section 28.1 & 28.2)

  • Dataset Name, Source, Version & License: DIODE (Dense Indoor and Outdoor DEpth, Vasiljevic et al., CoRR abs/1908.00463, 2019), obtained from the official release mirror (Kaggle Version 8) under the MIT License.
  • Genuine Cross-Modal Alignment: Every sample is captured from the exact same physical FARO Focus S350 laser scanner mount and timestamp, pairing an optical RGB photograph with a dense 3D laser range scan and binary return-validity mask. No synthetic or cross-dataset pairing is used.
  • Pair Volume & Threshold Compliance (Section 28.2): 7,195 aligned RGB–Depth pairs across 6 classes and 15 physical scenes (exceeding both the $\ge 5$ classes and $\ge 5,000$ paired-sample thresholds of Section 28.2).
  • Pair Integrity & Missing-Modality Audit: Automated verification in scripts/eda.py confirmed 100.0% file pairing (0 missing RGB, Depth, or Mask files across all 7,195 records). However, Modality B exhibits natural within-sample partial missingness: while indoor scenes average 98.1%–99.4% valid laser returns, outdoor mid- and high-elevation views lose 37.8%–37.9% of pixels (mean valid LiDAR rate 62.1%–62.2%) because laser pulses aimed at open sky never return.
  • Leakage-Free Scene-Level Split: Partitioned strictly by physical scene (`train`: 11 scenes, 4,995 pairs; `val`: 2 scenes, 1,100 pairs; `test`: 2 scenes, 1,100 pairs). All records from any given room or outdoor site reside in a single split (0% scene overlap; verified in proposal/scenes_used.csv).

3. Methodology & Multimodal Architecture (Handbook Section 29)

3.1 End-to-End Multimodal Pipeline

Raw Paired Data (RGB .jpg + LiDAR _depth.npy + _depth_mask.npy)
  → Preprocessing & Alignment (72×96 downsample; Modality A: ImageNet norm; Modality B: [log_d, mask, ∂z/∂y] + valid_ratio)
  → Synchronous DataLoader (joint horizontal flip in train; scene-disjoint splits)
  → Symmetric Modality Encoders (4-stage ConvNet: 3 → 32 → 64 → 96 → 128, yielding 3×4=12 spatial tokens + 128-d pooled vector)
  → Fusion Layer (Simple Late Concat vs. Bidirectional Cross-Modal Attention + Validity-Conditioned Reliability Gate)
  → Joint Multi-Task Loss (L_CE_fused + 0.3 * (L_CE_rgb + L_CE_depth) with p=0.15 training Modality Dropout)
  → Prediction & Evaluation (6-class logits → Macro-F1, Accuracy, Support/Conflict & Missing-Modality Audit)

3.2 Four Mandatory Models & Two Controlled Ablations (Section 29.1 & 29.2)

  1. Model 1 – Unimodal A Baseline (rgb_only, 463,974 params): 4-stage ConvNet encoder (`Conv2d-BN-GELU` $\times 2$ per stage, channels $3 \to 32 \to 64 \to 96 \to 128$) on RGB appearance only, followed by `AvgPool2d(3, 3)`, `LayerNorm`, global token averaging, and a linear classifier.
  2. Model 2 – Unimodal B Baseline (depth_only, 463,974 params): Identical 4-stage ConvNet backbone operating strictly on the 3-channel LiDAR geometry tensor $[\tilde{d}, m, \partial_y \tilde{d}]$. Invalid pixels ($m=0$) are zero-masked before gradient computation so sensor dropouts do not create artificial depth steps.
  3. Model 3 – Simple Fusion Baseline (simple_fusion, 960,326 params): Late concatenation of the pooled unimodal embeddings $[z_{\text{rgb}} \;;\; z_{\text{depth}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{256}$ projected through `Linear(256, 128) → LayerNorm → GELU → Dropout(0.2) → Linear(128, 6)` and trained with a single cross-entropy loss.
  4. Model 4 – Improved Multimodal Model (improved_fusion, 1,137,236 params): Addresses three core weaknesses of naive late concatenation:
    • Intermediate Bidirectional Cross-Modal Attention: Preserves $3 \times 4 = 12$ spatial region tokens per modality ($\mathbb{R}^{B \times 12 \times 128}$) with learnable 2D positional embeddings and applies 4-head cross-attention (`RGB queries ← Depth keys/values` and `Depth queries ← RGB keys/values`), scaled by a learnable residual gate $\alpha$ and the LiDAR valid-pixel ratio $r_{\text{valid}}$: $$z_{\text{rgb}} = \text{LN}\big(z_{\text{rgb}}^{\text{raw}} + \alpha_{\text{rgb}}\, r_{\text{valid}}\, \text{Attn}(T_{\text{rgb}}, T_{\text{depth}})\big), \quad z_{\text{depth}} = \text{LN}\big(z_{\text{depth}}^{\text{raw}} + \alpha_{\text{depth}}\, \text{Attn}(T_{\text{depth}}, T_{\text{rgb}})\big)$$
    • Validity-Conditioned Reliability Gate: Computes a channel-wise gate $g = \sigma(\text{MLP}([z_{\text{rgb}}, r_{\text{valid}} z_{\text{depth}}, r_{\text{valid}}])) \in (0,1)^{128}$ that dynamically shifts weight toward RGB when LiDAR returns are sparse ($r_{\text{valid}} \ll 1$), forming $z_{\text{fused}} = \text{MLP}([g \odot z_{\text{rgb}} \;;\; (1-g)r_{\text{valid}}z_{\text{depth}} \;;\; g \odot z_{\text{rgb}} + (1-g)r_{\text{valid}}z_{\text{depth}}]) $.
    • Auxiliary Unimodal Supervision & Modality Dropout (Missing-Modality Handling): Trains with $\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(\hat{y}_{\text{fused}}, y) + 0.3(\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(\hat{y}_{\text{rgb}}, y) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}(\hat{y}_{\text{depth}}, y))$ and drops the LiDAR branch with probability $p_{\text{drop}} = 0.15$ during training so neither encoder becomes lazy.
  5. Controlled Ablation 1 (ablation_no_gate, 978,258 params): Removes bidirectional cross-modal attention and the validity-conditioned gate ($g \equiv 0.5$) while keeping auxiliary unimodal supervision ($\lambda_{\text{aux}} = 0.3$) and modality dropout ($p_{\text{drop}} = 0.15$) fixed.
  6. Controlled Ablation 2 (ablation_no_aux, 1,137,236 params): Retains bidirectional cross-modal attention and the validity gate, but removes auxiliary unimodal supervision ($\lambda_{\text{aux}} = 0$) and training modality dropout ($p_{\text{drop}} = 0$).

6. Unimodal vs. Multimodal Comparison, Support/Conflict & Ablation Analysis

6.1 Why Unimodal B Excels Indoor While Improved Fusion Wins on Degraded-LiDAR Outdoor Classes (Section 29.1)

  • Why overall Macro-F1 favors Depth-Geometry (90.84%) on clean indoor scenes: As Handbook Section 29.1 notes, multimodal performance is not guaranteed to exceed unimodal performance when one modality is invariant to scene shift and the other is domain-sensitive. On indoor rooms (`98.1%–99.4%` valid LiDAR pixels), physical surface slant $\partial_y \tilde{d}$ is a direct geometric invariant of camera pitch (`96.68%–97.22%` F1 for `depth_only`). By contrast, `rgb_only` (`65.62%` Macro-F1) suffers from cross-room appearance shift because the test split contains a single unseen indoor building (`scene_00000`) whose wall colors and furnishings differ from the 5 training rooms.
  • Where Multimodal Fusion decisively beats both unimodal baselines: In `Outdoor-Mid (20-30°)` and `Outdoor-High (40-50°)` — where **37.8%–37.9% of LiDAR returns are lost to open sky** — `depth_only` degrades to `83.91%` and `73.63%` F1. Here, Improved Fusion achieves the highest F1 of any model: 85.27% on Outdoor-Mid (+1.36% over Depth, +13.56% over RGB) and 80.41% on Outdoor-High (+6.78% over Depth, +21.21% over RGB), and ranks #1 across all noisy-LiDAR test pairs (`<65%` valid returns, $n=200$) with **91.92% Macro-F1**.
  • Diagnosing "Modality Laziness" in Naive Simple Fusion (`71.30%` vs. `87.12%` / `90.64%`): Without auxiliary unimodal losses, `simple_fusion` (`71.30%`) and `ablation_no_aux` (`74.74%`) quickly minimize training loss by memorizing training-scene RGB textures, starving the Depth encoder of gradient signal. Adding auxiliary per-modality supervision ($\lambda_{\text{aux}} = 0.3$) and training modality dropout ($p_{\text{drop}} = 0.15$) boosts test Macro-F1 by **+12.38 percentage points** (`74.74% → 87.12%` in `improved_fusion`) and **+19.34 percentage points** (`71.30% → 90.64%` in `ablation_no_gate`).

  • Takeaway 1 (Auxiliary Loss + Modality Dropout is essential for missing-modality resilience): When LiDAR fails completely at inference (`Drop Depth at Test = 0% LiDAR`), `Simple Fusion` collapses to **3.41% Macro-F1** and `Ablation 2` drops to **23.59%**, whereas `Improved Fusion` (**59.36%**) and `Ablation 1` (**63.99%**) retain nearly the full accuracy of the standalone `RGB-Only` model (`65.62%`) without retraining.
  • Takeaway 2 (Cross-Attention + Validity Gate helps noisy outdoor LiDAR but slightly couples indoor features): Comparing `Improved Fusion` (`87.12%`) against `Ablation 1` (`90.64%`), spatial cross-attention + validity gating improves Macro-F1 on the noisy-LiDAR outdoor regime (`89.66% → 91.92%`), whereas on clean indoor LiDAR (`≥65%` valid), additive gating without cross-attention (`Ablation 1`, `87.99%`) avoids injecting unseen-room RGB style shift into the geometric branch.

7. Error Analysis

  • Adjacent-elevation boundary confusions dominate residual errors: Across all models, `96.4%–97.7%` of test samples are assigned to the correct environment domain (`Indoor` vs. `Outdoor`). Over `85%` of remaining errors occur between adjacent camera-elevation regimes (`Level 0–10° ↔ Mid 20–30°` and `Mid 20–30° ↔ High 40–50°`), where a $10^\circ$ pitch step (`010°` vs. `020°`, or `030°` vs. `040°`) changes the vertical horizon position by less than 15% of image height.
  • Canopy-occluded outdoor upward views (`Outdoor-High` → `Outdoor-Mid`): When an upward-tilted outdoor camera (`040–050°`) points into dense overhanging tree branches or a tall building facade rather than open sky, both RGB texture and LiDAR vertical gradient resemble `Outdoor-Mid (20–30°)` (Figure 4, Row 4).

8. Limitations and Conclusion

  • Scene count per evaluation split: Because the dataset is split strictly by physical scene to prevent data leakage, `val` and `test` each contain 2 scenes (`1` indoor, `1` outdoor). Consequently, RGB appearance experiences a stronger domain shift between splits than 3D LiDAR surface geometry.
  • Single-seed main benchmark: All six configurations were trained and evaluated under an identical deterministic protocol (`seed = 42`) on the same GPU; multi-seed runs can be launched directly via python train.py --config ... --seed 43.
  • Conclusion: Combining co-registered RGB appearance and 3D LiDAR geometry with auxiliary unimodal supervision, modality dropout, and validity-conditioned cross-attention improves Macro-F1 over naive late concatenation by +15.82 percentage points (`71.30% → 87.12%`, and `90.64%` with gated additive fusion), rescues 51.4% of samples where both unimodal baselines fail, and prevents catastrophic collapse under complete LiDAR sensor failure (`59.36%` vs. `3.41%` Macro-F1).
