1. Problem Statement

  • Task Goal: Monocular Depth Estimation — predict physical distance (in metres) from camera per pixel from a single 2D RGB photo.
  • Key Challenge: Scale ambiguity in 2D photos (a small nearby object vs. a giant distant object can take up identical pixel sizes). Model must learn visual perspective, texture gradients, and boundary cues.
  • Data Scope: Evaluated on both Indoor (1–9m close-range) and Outdoor (up to 100–200m long-range) scenes. Ground truth is measured by LiDAR laser scanners. Missing laser reflections (sky, glass, sharp edges) use a validity mask (1 = valid depth, 0 = ignore).

Input RGBSingle RGB photo, shape 3 × 288 × 384 (H × W)
Target DepthPer-pixel depth map in metres, shape 1 × 288 × 384
Ground Truth MaskBinary validity mask m (1 = valid pixel, 0 = ignore pixel)
Loss TargetCompute loss and evaluation metrics strictly on valid pixels (mask = 1)

2. Dataset Description & Preliminary EDA

2.1 Dataset Overview & Processing Workflow

  • Source Dataset: DIODE — Dense Indoor and Outdoor DEpth (Vasiljevic et al., 2019), diode-dataset.org. Obtained through a Kaggle mirror of the official release. The upstream release totals 26,229 RGB images (train 8,574 indoor + 16,884 outdoor; val 325 indoor + 446 outdoor), which matches the mirror we downloaded.
  • Version: the original 2019 DIODE release, obtained from the Kaggle mirror artemmmtry/diode-a-dense-indoor-and-outdoor-depth-dataset, Kaggle Version 8 (199.89 GB, last updated two years ago). Not DIODEv2: the mirror holds 26,229 images, exactly the 25,458 training plus 771 validation images of the 2019 release, and its data sources contain only train/ and val/.
  • License: MIT, and consistent at both levels, which was checked rather than assumed: the upstream release states MIT on diode-dataset.org and in the official devkit repository diode-dataset/diode-devkit, and the Kaggle mirror we downloaded declares MIT in its own License field. The mirror therefore imposes no terms beyond the upstream licence, and redistribution of a derived subset is permitted provided the citation below is kept.
  • Citation: I. Vasiljevic, N. Kolkin, S. Zhang, R. Luo, H. Wang, F. Z. Dai, A. F. Daniele, M. Mostajabi, S. Basart, M. R. Walter and G. Shakhnarovich. DIODE: A Dense Indoor and Outdoor DEpth Dataset. CoRR abs/1908.00463, 2019.
  • Sample Volume: 7,195 RGB–Depth pairs (4,995 train, 1,100 val, 1,100 test across 15 scenes).
  • Sample Modality: Each sample consists of an RGB image, a high-precision laser depth map in metres, and a binary validity mask indicating measured laser reflections.
  • Processing Rule: Raw data is loaded dynamically during training via PyTorch DataLoaders; raw data files are kept local and excluded from repository version control.

2.2 Data Splitting Strategy (Scene-Level Split)

  • Leakage Prevention: Data is partitioned strictly by Scene (physical location / room) rather than random image sampling. This ensures no overlapping scenes exist between training and evaluation splits, preventing memory shortcut leakage.
  • Split Ratio: ~70% Train / 15% Val / 15% Test (seed 42). Verified to have 0% scene overlap across splits.
  • Provenance of the three splits. DIODE has not released a public test set (the official devkit lists it as "coming soon"), and the official validation set holds only 771 images, fewer than our validation split alone. All three splits below are therefore carved from the official DIODE training set, partitioned by scene. This is a deliberate choice and it has a consequence we state rather than hide: our numbers are not comparable to published DIODE benchmark results, which use the official protocol.
  • Subset selection rule. The upstream release holds 26,229 images; the subset takes 7,195 of them under a fixed rule, implemented in scripts/make_subset.py and reproducible from seed 42:
    1. Collect every RGB file under train/<domain>/<scene>/<scan>/ that has both a depth map and a validity mask.
    2. Shuffle the scene list, then take at most 700 images per indoor scene until 4,000 are collected, and at most 400 per outdoor scene until 3,000 are collected. The per-scene cap is what spreads the subset over many scenes instead of exhausting one. A scene holding fewer images than the cap contributes all it has, which is why the per-scene counts in the table below are uneven (466, 389, 340) rather than all equal to the cap.
    3. This selects all 7 indoor scenes and 8 of the 12 outdoor scenes.
    4. Reseed, shuffle the selected scenes again, and partition them 70 / 15 / 15 by scene count, not by image count, giving 5 / 1 / 1 indoor and 6 / 1 / 1 outdoor.
  • Scene manifest: the scene-to-split assignment is committed as proposal/scenes_used.csv and is regenerated from the data by scripts/export_scenes.py, which fails if any scene appears in more than one split. That script is the evidence for the no-leakage claim above.

2.3 Visual Inspection & Data Characteristics

  • Visual Alignment: Nearby ceiling lamps and pipes display in cool blue; far background walls shift to dark red. Outdoor paths gradually transition from blue to red (~40–50m).
  • Edge Noise (Edge Bleeding): Indoor invalid pixels appear mainly as thin boundary outlines around sharp object contours where laser reflection drops.
  • Outdoor Sky Region: Sky, fine branches, and objects beyond sensor range are masked as invalid (e.g. crane photo is mostly sky, leaving 6.9% valid pixels).

  • What the resampling correction changed. Comparing the rebuilt figures against the earlier bilinear build isolates the effect, and it behaves exactly as the mechanism predicts:
    • Indoor is unchanged (p5, median and p95 identical to the previous digit; p99 20.2 to 20.1 m). Indoor scenes carry 0.2 to 0.8 percent invalid pixels, so there was almost nothing for the interpolation to contaminate.
    • Outdoor depths moved upward, most at the low end where the contamination concentrates: validation p5 rose 1.4 to 2.1 m and its median 7.1 to 7.7 m. Bilinear had been dragging those values down toward the zeros of the sky.
    • The share of invalid pixels fell: 31.4 to 30.3 percent for outdoor training, 47.4 to 44.4 for validation, 33.2 to 31.8 for test, while indoor barely moved. This is the clearest evidence of the defect. The mask was resampled with nearest in both builds and is therefore byte-identical, so the drop cannot come from the mask. It comes from valid pixels whose depth had been averaged down to exactly zero and were then rejected by the depth > 0 test. On the outdoor validation split that was about 1.3 million pixels of ground truth silently discarded, and the loss scaled with the invalid fraction of each split.
  • Depth bounds applied at load time. Pixels beyond 50 m indoor or 200 m outdoor are masked out by setting mask = 0. Measured on the training split, these two bounds do very different things, and the distinction is worth stating rather than hiding behind one number:
    • The indoor bound removes nothing. No training image has a single valid pixel above 50 m (0 of 2,606 images, 0.0000% of valid pixels). This matches the 50 m indoor range DIODE documents for its sensor, so the bound is a guard against out-of-range values rather than a filter that acts on this data.
    • The outdoor bound does cut real measurements. It touches 149 of 2,389 training images and 0.0048% of valid pixels. Those are not sensor artifacts: DIODE documents a 300 m outdoor range, and nothing in the subset exceeds it (0 of 2,389 images above 300 m). The 200 m bound is therefore a deliberate choice to exclude the sparse long tail, which keeps the loss from being dominated by a handful of distant pixels, not a noise filter.
  • Camera Viewpoint Finding: DIODE camera tilt codes cover 000 to 050 only (level or upward view; no downward floor-facing views). This is an inherent property of DIODE source dataset.

3. Methodology

3.1 Preprocessing Pipeline

  • Image Resizing: Resized from 1024×768 down to 384×288, about one seventh of the pixels, to keep the subset small enough to hold locally and to fit the training budget in Section 4.2. The resampling filter differs by array, and the distinction matters: the RGB image is a feature and is resized bilinearly, then stored as JPEG at quality 90; the depth map and the validity mask are labels and are resampled with nearest neighbour only. Depth is stored as float16 and the mask as uint8.
  • Why depth must not be interpolated. DIODE stores an invalid pixel as 0. Averaging depth across a resize mixes those zeros into neighbouring valid pixels and pulls them downward, producing distances that no surface occupies — precisely in the edge-bleeding region described in Section 2.3. Because the mask is resampled with nearest, it stays clean and does not mark the affected pixels, so a pixel can read mask = 1 while its depth has been contaminated. Outdoor scenes carry 31 to 47 percent invalid pixels, so a large share of their boundaries are exposed to this.
  • RGB Normalization: Scaled to [0, 1] range and normalized using standard ImageNet mean/std.
  • Depth Target Processing: Maintained in true physical metres; invalid pixels and extreme depth outliers (> 50m/200m) are masked out during loss calculation.
  • Data Augmentation: Synchronous Random Horizontal Flip (p = 0.5) applied simultaneously to RGB, depth, and validity mask.

3.2 Baseline & Pretrained Models

  • Baseline Model: Lightweight U-Net (encoder–decoder with skip connections) trained from scratch.
  • Pretrained Model: MiDaS Small (EfficientNet-Lite3 backbone, 21.3M parameters), predicting inverse depth (`1 / output`) trained with masked SILog loss.
  • Loss Function: Masked Scale-Invariant Logarithmic (SILog) loss (Eigen et al., 2014) computed over valid pixels.

3.3 Evaluation Metrics

  • Abs Rel (Absolute Relative Error): `(1/N) * sum(|d - d*| / d*)` → Lower is better (↓).
  • RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error): `sqrt((1/N) * sum((d - d*)^2))` → Lower is better (↓).
  • Threshold Accuracy (δ < 1.25k): % of valid pixels with `max(d/d*, d*/d) < 1.25^k` for k = 1, 2, 3 → Higher is better (↑).

4. Experimental Setup

4.1 Controlled Experiment Design

  • Research Question: Does full network fine-tuning (Option B) significantly outperform encoder-frozen fine-tuning (Option A)?
  • Variable Factor: Option A: Frozen encoder (decoder only) vs. Option B: Full fine-tuning (all layers updated).
  • Controlled Factors: Identical dataset split, preprocessing, SILog loss, AdamW optimizer, batch size 8, 20 epochs.
  • Runs & Seeds: 3 independent random seeds (42, 43, 44), reporting Mean ± Standard Deviation.
  • Hypothesis: full fine-tuning adapts the MiDaS encoder to the DIODE depth scale and sensor characteristics, which a frozen encoder cannot, so Option B achieves lower Abs Rel than Option A.
  • Decision criterion: Option B is judged better than Option A only if its mean test Abs Rel is lower and the two mean ± 1 standard deviation intervals over the three seeds do not overlap. If the intervals overlap, the result is reported as no measurable difference at n = 3, not as a win for either option. The same rule is applied to RMSE and δ < 1.25 as secondary checks; Abs Rel decides.
  • Determinism: each run pins its seed across random, numpy and torch and sets torch.backends.cudnn.deterministic = True with the cuDNN autotuner disabled. Without that last step a convolutional encoder does not reproduce from its seed, so the seed-to-seed spread this experiment measures would be confounded by run-to-run noise.

4.2 Hardware & Speed Benchmark

  • Hardware: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 Laptop GPU (8 GB VRAM).
  • Inference Speed (Batch 8): 4.9 ms / image (~205 FPS), using 0.42 GB VRAM.
  • Training Speed (FP32, Batch 8): ~1.5 min / epoch (3.2 GB VRAM).
  • Training Speed (AMP Mixed Precision, Batch 8): ~1.0 min / epoch (2.0 GB VRAM).
  • Total Compute Budget: 6 runs × 20 epochs = ~3 GPU hours. Total estimate including baseline & 2× thermal throttling buffer: ~6–8 GPU hours (runs locally on laptop).

8. Limitations and Conclusion

8.1 Dataset & Pipeline Limitations

  1. Val and test sets each contain only 1 indoor scene and 1 outdoor scene.
  2. Test indoor room is smaller than train rooms (median 1.8m vs 2.6m).
  3. Outdoor val set has a higher sky/invalid pixel percentage (~47% vs ~31% in train).
  4. Laser ground-truth depth has minor noise at sharp object boundaries (edge bleeding).
  5. DIODE lacks downward floor-facing camera tilt (vertical angles cover 000–050 only).
  6. Downsampling to 384×288 smooths fine structures like distant wires and thin branches.
  7. All three splits are carved from the official DIODE training set because no public test set exists, so results here cannot be compared against published DIODE benchmark numbers.
  8. The outdoor cap of 400 images per scene is tighter than the indoor cap of 700, so the subset is deliberately not proportional to the upstream domain sizes; outdoor scene diversity was preferred over outdoor image count.
  9. RGB images are stored as JPEG at quality 90 rather than lossless PNG, a size trade-off that introduces compression artifacts the upstream release does not have.
  10. Depth is stored as float16. At the outdoor range this quantises to roughly 0.06 m near 100 m and 0.13 m near 200 m, which is below the noise floor of the sensor at those distances but is not exact.

8.2 M1 Conclusion

Our dataset subset is verified clean and free of scene leakage. Local GPU benchmarks confirm fast fine-tuning (~1 min/epoch with AMP), demonstrating that our planned controlled experiment is completely feasible on local hardware within 6–8 GPU hours.
